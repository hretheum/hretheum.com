We HAVE to talk about ChatGPT and how it might be quietly changing our brains.

This study from MIT’s Media Lab is one of the first neuroscience investigations into how generative AI affects us.

It actually confirms something we’ve known for a long time: the brain remembers what it actively performs. If you write something by hand, you’ll remember it better than if you type. If you go on a trip without taking pictures, the memory sticks more than when you document every moment. Automation, on the other hand, gives the brain permission to offload the information and not hold on to it.

ChatGPT looks like the newest example of this. By automating parts of thinking, it lets the brain offload memory, effort, and engagement.

⸻

So Here’s What They Did 

Methodology
 • The study ran for 4 months
 • More than 50 participants were involved
 • Researchers measured:
 • Brain scans (fMRI and EEG) to track neural activity
 • Memory recall tests (immediate sentence recall)
 • Writing samples reviewed by educators
 • Participants wrote in three different conditions: (a) On their own (b) with Google Search (c) with ChatGPT

⸻

So, let’s talk about the results. What did they find?

The Findings

1. Memory Loss in Action

 • 83% of ChatGPT users could not recall a single sentence they had written just minutes earlier.
 • People writing without AI had no trouble remembering.

2. Brain Power Drops

 • Brain connectivity fell from 79 to 42 points in ChatGPT users.
 • That’s a 47% decrease in neural engagement—the lowest of all groups.
 • Even when they stopped using AI, these participants stayed less engaged than those who never used it.

3. Writing Quality Suffers

 • Essays written with ChatGPT were called “robotic,” “soulless,” and “lacking depth.”
 • AI users wrote in more repetitive, formulaic ways.

4. Speed vs. Learning

 • ChatGPT made people much faster at finishing tasks.
 • But the mental effort that builds learning dropped by about one-third.
 • Translation: you gain speed, but lose depth.

5. The Best Approach

 • The top-performing group began writing without AI and only added ChatGPT later.
 • They kept stronger memory, better brain activity, and higher-quality work.

⸻

The Takeaway

Automation has always reduced what our brains remember. This study shows ChatGPT is no exception. It makes us quicker, but at the cost of memory, depth, and engagement.

The solution isn’t to avoid AI altogether. The solution is to use it intentionally:
 • Start with your own thinking
 • Bring AI in later to polish or expand
 • Use it as a tool, not a substitute for thought

MIT’s early research is a wake-up call. The way we use AI will matter more than ever.
